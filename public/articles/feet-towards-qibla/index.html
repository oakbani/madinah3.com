1 · The ruling at a glance

Disliked, but not a sin

Qibla is the direction of the Kaʿbah, which Muslims face in prayer. Makruh means disliked: it is better to avoid, but the one who does it is not sinful.

The ruling

Pointing the feet toward the qibla is makruh (disliked). It is not a sin.

There is no verse or hadith that speaks about the feet directly. The dislike comes from respect for the qibla, based on the Qurʾan and on hadith about the qibla.
2 · Disliked or allowed?

Scholars have two views

Neither view calls it a sin. They differ on one point: is it disliked, or simply allowed?

ForbiddenHaram
DislikedMakruh
AllowedMubah
RecommendedMustahabb

The two views among the scholars are shaded.

Makruh
مَكْرُوه

Disliked. It is better to avoid, out of respect for the qibla. This is the position written in the Hanafi books, and Hanbali scholars report it.

Mubah
مُبَاح

Allowed. There is no clear proof, so there is nothing wrong in it.

This guide follows the first view: it is disliked, because of the Qurʾanic principle of honouring what is sacred, and the hadith about the qibla. But because nothing in the Qurʾan or hadith speaks about the feet themselves, it is not a sin.

3 · Why is it disliked?

The verse, the hadith and the reasoning

The verse and hadith below do not mention the feet. Scholars compare: if the qibla must be respected in these cases, it should be respected here too.

Qurʾan Honouring what is sacred shows a God-fearing heart

ذَٰلِكَ وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ شَعَـٰٓئِرَ ٱللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى ٱلْقُلُوبِ
"That [is so]. And whoever honors the symbols [i.e., rites] of Allāh - indeed, it is from the piety of hearts."
al-Ḥajj 22:32 · Translation: Saheeh International (Quran.com)

Hadith The qibla must not be faced for these acts

إِذَا أَتَيْتُمُ الْغَائِطَ فَلَا تَسْتَقْبِلُوا الْقِبْلَةَ وَلَا تَسْتَدْبِرُوهَا بِبَوْلٍ وَلَا غَائِطٍ، وَلَكِنْ شَرِّقُوا أَوْ غَرِّبُوا
"When you go to relieve yourselves, do not face the qibla and do not turn your back to it, for urine or stool; face east or west."
Narrated by Abu Ayyub al-Anṣārī · Muslim · link · al-Bukhārī 394 · link
إِذَا كَانَ أَحَدُكُمْ يُصَلِّي، فَلَا يَبْصُقْ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ، فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ إِذَا صَلَّى
"When one of you is praying, he must not spit in front of him, for Allah is before his face when he prays." (The Prophet ﷺ said this after he saw spit on the wall of the qibla and scraped it off.)
Narrated by Ibn ʿUmar · al-Bukhārī 406 · link

Hadith A hadith that stresses it

مَنْ تَفَلَ تُجَاهَ الْقِبْلَةِ جَاءَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ تَفْلُهُ بَيْنَ عَيْنَيْهِ
"Whoever spits in the direction of the qibla will come on the Day of Resurrection with his spit between his eyes."
Narrated by Ḥudhayfa · Abu Dawud 3824 · link · Ibn Khuzayma 1663 · link

So the scholars reason in three steps

1

The Qurʾan teaches us to honour what is sacred

Honour is a mark of a God-fearing heart (22:32).

2

The hadith protect the direction of the qibla

The Prophet ﷺ taught us not to face it for the toilet or to spit toward it. He gave this respect to the direction itself, not only to the Kaʿbah.

3

Custom and comparison

Pointing the feet at something is widely seen as a lack of respect. Scholars compare it with the cases in the hadith, and so call it disliked.

Result: disliked (makruh), not a sin.No verse or hadith speaks about the feet directly. The dislike comes from comparison and custom, so it does not reach the level of sin.
4 · Be gentle, and avoid misunderstanding

Do not judge. Be wise.

Do not call others sinners

In many Arab countries, sitting with the feet toward the qibla is very common. People do it without any thought of disrespect. Do not call them sinners or accuse them of bad manners.

Leaving an adab is not always be-adabi.Adab means good manners. Missing a courtesy is not the same as being disrespectful. Intention and custom matter. Even the Hanafi books speak of doing it deliberately, without excuse (al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr).

Where it is seen as very disrespectful, avoid it

If you live where people feel strongly about this, do not do it. Do not do it to "prove a point". Guard your brothers and sisters from thinking badly of you.

The Prophet ﷺ cared about how things looked

Two Ansari men passed by while the Prophet ﷺ was walking with his wife Safiyya. He stopped them and said who she was, because he feared Shaytan would put an evil thought in their hearts.

عَلَى رِسْلِكُمَا، إِنَّهَا صَفِيَّةُ بِنْتُ حُيَيٍّ… إِنَّ الشَّيْطَانَ يَجْرِي مِنَ الْإِنْسَانِ مَجْرَى الدَّمِ، وَإِنِّي خَشِيتُ أَنْ يَقْذِفَ فِي قُلُوبِكُمَا شَرًّا - أَوْ قَالَ: شَيْئًا
"Take it easy, she is Safiyya bint Ḥuyayy… Shaytan runs in a person like blood, and I feared he might cast something evil into your hearts." (or he said "something")
Narrated by Ṣafiyya · Muslim · link · al-Bukhārī · link
5 · The schools’ own books

What the Hanafi and Hanbali books say

Hanafi Makruh

Makruh to stretch the feet toward the qibla, in sleep or otherwise, when done deliberately. The reason given: it leaves out respect for the qibla, and it is bad manners.

وَيُكْرَهُ مَدُّ الرِّجْلَيْنِ إِلَى الْقِبْلَةِ فِي النَّوْمِ وَغَيْرِهِ عَمْدًا
"It is disliked to stretch the feet toward the qibla, in sleep or otherwise, deliberately."
al-Nihāya fī Sharḥ al-Hidāya 3/99 · link
لِمَا فِيهِ مِنْ تَرْكِ تَعْظِيمِ جِهَةِ الْقِبْلَةِ
"Because it leaves out respect for the direction of the qibla."
al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī 5/321 · link
لِأَنَّهُ إسَاءَةُ أَدَبٍ
"Because it is bad manners."
Radd al-Muḥtār (Fatāwā Shāmī) 1/655 · link
Hanbali Disliked near the Kaʿbah; doubts it in general

Ibn Muflih reports the Hanafi view. He accepts it at the Kaʿbah itself. As a general rule, he says dislike needs proof, and that leaving it is probably better.

فَالْكَرَاهَةُ تَسْتَدْعِي دَلِيلًا شَرْعِيًّا … وَلَعَلَّ تَرْكَهُ أَوْلَى
"Dislike needs a legal proof… and perhaps leaving it is better."
Ibn Muflih, al-Ādāb al-Sharʿiyya 3/410 · link
